# Poseidon Product Vision

## One-line vision

**Poseidon is a beautiful personal atlas of your underwater life.**

It turns every recreational dive into a durable memory and every marine-life encounter into part of an accumulating personal collection.

## User

The primary user is the owner and a small circle of people close to them.

Poseidon is not initially a public-growth product. It does not need to prove market fit, acquire a niche, attract investors, maximize retention, or justify a subscription business. Product decisions should be judged first by whether they create a delightful, useful personal record that is worth maintaining for years.

## Product purpose

Poseidon should make it easy to:

- record every dive without technical paperwork;
- remember where it happened and what was seen;
- build a visual marine-life collection over time;
- revisit dives as memories rather than database rows;
- see the geographic shape of a diving life;
- accumulate meaningful milestones without turning safe diving into a game of optimization.

The **dive** is the canonical historical record.

The **creatures** are the emotional centre.

The **atlas** is the long-term artifact.

## Product thesis

Most dive-log software optimizes for technical completeness, computer import, certification evidence, or dive statistics.

Poseidon should optimize for **memory, delight and personal history**.

That means a dive log should answer not only:

- Where did I dive?
- How deep?
- How long?

but especially:

- What did I see?
- What was memorable?
- Where else have I seen that animal?
- Which creatures and places now make up my diving history?

## Product ancestry: Liebestraum

Poseidon should deliberately learn from `BenWassa/liebestraum`.

The transferable philosophy is:

1. Treat accumulated personal history as the main character.
2. Make creation quick and familiar.
3. Let real places, dates, encounters and milestones provide the delight.
4. Concentrate personality in meaningful moments rather than decorating every control.
5. Make the phone experience primary.
6. Turn accumulated entries into an artifact that is enjoyable to revisit.

Poseidon should not inherit Liebestraum's exact UI or backend architecture. It needs its own aquatic visual identity and interaction model.

## Experience principles

### 1. Delight first

Creature selection, dive completion, collection growth and revisiting history should feel unusually polished.

Performance work exists to protect delight, not to flatten it into a utilitarian form.

### 2. Every dive counts

Poseidon is a complete personal log, not only a highlight reel.

Even an ordinary dive belongs because lifetime totals, site history and encounter history become more valuable when the record is complete.

### 3. Creature-first memory

Marine life should receive more visual and interaction emphasis than technical dive metrics.

Depth and duration matter; creatures are what make one dive distinct from another.

### 4. Visual selection before text entry

Logging creatures should begin as a gallery of illustrated creatures with common names.

For a curated region, likely local creatures should lead. Search and arbitrary entry remain available so the gallery never becomes a constraint.

### 5. Content availability must never prevent logging

This is a permanent product law.

A diver may add a creature even if Poseidon does not yet have:

- curated artwork;
- canonical taxonomy;
- rarity data;
- regional metadata.

Missing enrichment can be added later without damaging the original dive record.

### 6. Common language first

The main UI should use names a recreational diver naturally recognizes.

Scientific names and deeper taxonomy may exist as metadata, but should not dominate the primary interaction.

### 7. Personal history before social mechanics

Poseidon does not need a feed, followers, likes or comments.

Sharing can eventually exist as an output from the personal record, not as the core product loop.

### 8. No fake rarity

Do not invent precise encounter probabilities.

Rarity or encounter significance may eventually use trustworthy regional evidence or carefully described editorial tiers. If the evidence is weak, omit the claim.

### 9. Never gamify unsafe behavior

Do not reward:

- maximum depth;
- maximum duration;
- decompression exposure;
- touching or approaching wildlife;
- dangerous conditions;
- breaking safe limits.

Milestones should celebrate accumulated experience, exploration and observation.

### 10. Offline is part of the experience

A dive journal that fails on boats, islands or weak hotel Wi-Fi is not reliable enough.

The core logging and browsing experience must tolerate no network connection.

## Visual character

Poseidon should be:

- light mode first;
- rich in ocean blues;
- supported by aquatic greens;
- accented with coral, tropical fish and reef colours;
- fluid and alive;
- polished and tactile;
- playful without becoming childish;
- premium without becoming sterile.

Creature artwork should feel collectible and desirable in its own right.

Avoid:

- dark technical-instrument styling;
- military/industrial scuba aesthetics;
- generic analytics dashboards;
- dense equipment forms;
- childish cartoon overload;
- decorative motion that slows logging;
- endless card-grid clutter without hierarchy.

## Long-term product shape

### Dive Journal

A complete chronological record of every dive.

### Marine Collection

Every creature encountered, linked back to the dives and locations where it was seen.

### Ocean Atlas

A geographic view of countries, regions and dive sites explored over time.

### Dive Detail

A single dive rendered as a memorable artifact: place, date, core stats, highlight encounter, other creatures and notes.

### Creature Detail

Artwork, common name, first encounter, most recent encounter, locations and dives where the creature was seen.

### Trips

Optional grouping of multiple dives into a travel episode such as `Cozumel — 2026`.

### Milestones

Restrained markers such as:

- first logged dive;
- 10 / 25 / 50 dives;
- first new country or region;
- first encounter with a major creature group;
- collection growth in a region;
- meaningful breadth of places explored.

Milestones are secondary to the journal itself.

## Initial content geography

Start with high-quality curated content for:

- Cozumel;
- Playa del Carmen;
- the Mexican Caribbean.

Poseidon must still allow arbitrary dives and arbitrary creature names anywhere in the world from day one.

Curated regions improve the gallery and metadata; they do not define the boundaries of the product.

## Explicit anti-goals for the first product

Poseidon is not initially:

- a PADI integration;
- a certification wallet;
- a dive-shop platform;
- a technical dive planner;
- a decompression tool;
- a gas-management tool;
- a dive-computer analytics suite;
- a social network;
- a photo-management product;
- a global scientific taxonomy project;
- a marketplace.

## Product success

The first success test is personal:

> After a real dive day, is Poseidon the place I naturally want to record the dives, and is the resulting history enjoyable enough that I want to keep it complete?

A second test follows later:

> After dozens of dives, does the accumulated journal, collection and atlas feel substantially more valuable than the individual entries did when they were created?

# Creature art biological QA — 2026-09-09

Issue: #12

## Outcome

The initial 30-candidate editorial library is now 22 `keep`, 0 `provisional`, 8 `remake`. All six provisional states were resolved. The seven original remake targets were regenerated/reviewed; none was allowed to displace its source revision without a biologically reliable, durable source binary.

### Resolved provisional assets

- Nurse shark — **keep / high**: barbels, broad flattened head, rear-set dorsals.
- Stoplight parrotfish — **keep / high**: robust beak/body and usable terminal-phase pattern.
- Caribbean reef squid — **keep / high**: elongate mantle and full-length lateral fins.
- Caribbean reef octopus — **remake / medium**: generic octopus identity is insufficient for `Octopus briareus`.
- Trumpetfish — **keep / high**: elongate body, tubular snout, posterior fins.
- Longsnout seahorse — **keep / medium**: long snout/slender form; colour variability prevents overstating confidence.

### Remake attempts retained as blocked

- Spotted eagle ray — replacement generation improved the diagnostic direction, but no reviewed replacement binary was committed.
- Spotted trunkfish — rejected: reversed adult spot pattern.
- Atlantic blue tang — rejected: repeatedly drifted toward Indo-Pacific blue-tang morphology.
- Spanish hogfish — rejected: adult colour division unreliable.
- Caribbean cushion sea star — rejected: body/arms insufficiently inflated and cushion-like.
- Porkfish — rejected: bar/stripe pattern unreliable.
- Queen conch — rejected: shell-only treatment omits useful visible soft anatomy.

### Expansion

`Sanopus splendidus` was attempted first because `splendid-toadfish` already exists in the content pack and is particularly relevant to Cozumel. The generated result was rejected as a generic toadfish and was **not** added as a source candidate. This avoids both wrong art and silent taxonomy creation.

## Reference basis

The pass used species-level reference material rather than image aesthetics as authority: Smithsonian Tropical Research Institute Caribbean fish records, Florida Museum species accounts, NOAA Fisheries queen-conch material, Smithsonian/Caribbean material for the cushion sea star, and the existing content-pack provenance. No rarity or encounter-frequency claim was inferred from these references.

## Promotion boundary

No runtime asset was changed. `assets/source` remains editorial input; `assets/creatures` remains canonical runtime output. #11's `keep`-only promotion gate, source-ID mapping, explicit ingestion mode and atomic replacement requirements remain intact.
